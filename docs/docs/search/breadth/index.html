Search strategies

Breadth search, RDR(2, m)

The root and m independently observed candidates. Each of the root's top-m documents is appended to the root once, and the m states are encoded in one batch. The global readout weights the root and the mean of the m observations equally (Eq. 4 of the paper).

A low-cost operating point for single-hop retrieval

On single-hop suites, two batched rounds already improve on the paired root by 0.77, 1.51 and 1.59 mean nDCG@10 at m = 2, 8 and 16 (Table 2, separate single-observation configuration with a 2048-token query budget):

Width does not replace depth on multi-hop

At matched maximum budgets the tree wins on every multi-hop suite. RDR(3, 3) beats RDR(2, 12) at 13 states, and RDR(4, 3) beats RDR(2, 39) at 40 states by 5.87 to 13.22 Recall@5 (Table 15). Evidence that is only retrievable after an observation needs a second level.

Breadth saturates early on single-hop suites: moving from 8 to 39 observations changes BRIGHT by +0.16 and NanoBEIR by 0.00 nDCG@10 (Table 16).

classrdr.BreadthSearch(width=8, dedup='level')

Shallow breadth RDR(2, m): the root and m independently observed candidates (Eq. 4), 1 + m encodings in two dependent rounds.

Parameters

  • widthintdefault 8

    m, the root's top-m candidates; each is observed by one level-2 state.

Advanced parameters (1)
  • dedupstrdefault 'level'

    Selection rule, as in TreeSearch (only the root selects, so the modes coincide).
